Nuclear quantum effects broaden and shift electronic absorption bands, but including them at an affordable cost is still a challenge for condensed-phase chromophores. In this work we sample acrolein configurations with the adaptive quantum thermal bath (adQTB) and with path-integral molecular dynamics, in the gas phase and in aqueous solution, and compute the resulting steady-state spectra from *ab initio* excited-state calculations. The comparison shows where a quantum-thermal-bath description of the nuclei is reliable for spectroscopy and what it costs relative to path integrals.
