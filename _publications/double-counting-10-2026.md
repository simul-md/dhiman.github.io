Mixed quantum–classical simulations in which a tunnelling coordinate is propagated as a wavefunction and its environment as classical trajectories under a quantum thermal bath (QTB), count the zero-point energy twice: the wavefunction carries it intrinsically and the QTB noise supplies it again. This work removes this double counting by partitioning the thermostat spectrum, assigning its zero-point part to the wavefunction and driving the bath with the thermal part only. In the harmonic Caldeira–Leggett limit the partitioned scheme reproduces the exact variance to 0.3–1.1%, with a residual given in closed form, whereas the unpartitioned hybrid (wavefunction coupled to a standard QTB) is off by a factor of two. In a dissipative double well the partition removes the zero-point heating and holds the quantum energy at 100 K; the heating that remains at 300 K is traced to the mean-field back-reaction.
